# Rank retrieval by explicit query purpose

The Codex Archive stores evidence, provenance, relationships, determinations,
and dispositions without a universal credibility order. Each Knowledge
Dispensary query instead executes under a Retrieval Contract that declares its
purpose, scope, time view, relevant Verification Requirements, Evidence Grade
facet priorities, filters, and presentation constraints. Ranking is a derived
query result, not stored authority.

At minimum, the contract distinguishes these retrieval modes:

- exploratory discovery prioritizes topical reach, diversity, and proposed
  connections while keeping uncertainty and source roles visible;
- current status prioritizes applicable current determinations, scope fit,
  freshness, and compact conflict markers;
- conflict review prioritizes competing claims, contested dimensions,
  defeaters, and complete parallel histories without selecting a winner;
- formal verification prioritizes complete Verification Routes, Evidence
  Anchors, receipts, required Evidence Grade facets, and unresolved gaps; and
- historical or as-of reconstruction prioritizes evidence and determinations
  available at the declared time without hindsight.

The same evidence item may rank differently under different contracts without
its Evidence Grade, Claim Status, provenance, or disposition changing. No mode
may convert rank, popularity, semantic similarity, venue, or a UI badge into a
determination. A derived ordering must retain the contract and its explanation,
including the facet priorities and tie-breaks used.

Every query's Honest Search Receipt binds the Retrieval Contract to the corpus
freshness epochs, searched and unavailable sources, filters, candidate coverage,
ranking derivation, and returned result identifiers. This makes two differently
ranked answers comparable and keeps cached results auditable without pretending
that one global ordering fits discovery, current status, conflict review, and
verification equally well.
